Why hollow core

Solid glass fiber has hit its limits

Conventional fiber guides light through solid silica. Hollow-core fiber guides it through air, held in place by a ring of ultra-thin glass capillaries.

Loss floor

Standard fiber has been stuck near 0.14 dB/km since the 1980s.

Latency

Glass slows light to about two-thirds of its speed in air.

Nonlinearity & noise

Raman scattering limits power and drowns out fragile quantum signals.

0.091 dB/km

Record loss for hollow-core DNANF fiber (2025): lower than any glass fiber

~30%

Lower latency than standard silica fiber

Band with loss under 0.2 dB/km, with ~7× lower dispersion

Our products

Precision pre-forms, built from the glass up

A hollow-core pre-form is an assembly of precision quartz capillaries inside a jacket tube. Its geometry decides the fiber's loss.

Anti-resonant pre-forms

Nested (NANF) and double-nested (DNANF) capillary designs for ultra-low loss.

Photonic bandgap stacks

Stack-and-draw honeycomb pre-forms and canes.

Custom R&D batches

Small lots and bespoke geometries for labs, IITs and quantum start-ups.

Precision quartz tubing

Thin-wall capillaries, jacket tubes, joining and machining.

How we make a pre-form

Hollow-core pre-forms are built by mechanical assembly, not chemical deposition — a craft that rewards decades of glass-working skill.

1

Quartz tube selection

High-purity, low-OH synthetic silica

2

Capillary drawing

Thin walls drawn to tight tolerances

3

Nest & stack

Capillaries positioned inside the jacket

4

Fuse & cane

Tack-fused, caned and sleeved into the pre-form

5

Fiber draw

Pressure-controlled draw at partner tower

Steps 1–4 are done in-house. Fiber drawing is done with partners, with in-house drawing planned.

Quantum applications

The quiet channel for quantum

QKD alongside live data

Very low Raman noise lets quantum keys share a fiber with high-power classical traffic.

Entanglement networks

Low loss and low dispersion preserve photon states between nodes.

Timing & clock sync

About 30% lower latency for quantum and 6G timing.

Gas-filled quantum devices

Atomic-vapour-filled cores for quantum memories and frequency standards.

Sensing applications

Sensing with an air core

Fiber gyroscopes

Far less sensitive to temperature and magnetic fields: steadier navigation for defence, space and drones.

Gas sensing

Gas fills the core itself, giving long interaction lengths for methane, hydrogen and CO₂ detection.

High-power lasers

Air core carries intense and ultrafast pulses for surgery, micromachining and LIDAR.

Harsh environments

Minimal glass in the light path suits radiation, nuclear and space use.
